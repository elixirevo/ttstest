The world’s first open-source cryptocurrency, developed by Satoshi Nakamoto. It employs a peer-to-peer (P2P) proof-of-work mechanism.

Its unofficial codes are “XBT” or “BTC,” and in South Korea, it is commonly abbreviated as “Bitco” or “Bit.” Since cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin are not legal tender, they do not have codes standardized by organizations like ISO. The most commonly used three-letter abbreviation, “BTC,” is simply an abbreviation for “BiTCoin,” while “XBT” follows the ISO 4217 naming convention but is also not an official code.

2. Features[edit] Most exchanges, including this document, refer only to the blockchain running the “Bitcoin Core” client—led by Bitcoin developers—as “Bitcoin.”

Unlike traditional currencies, it enables fast and secure peer-to-peer transactions without the intervention of governments, central banks, or financial institutions. A key feature is that, unlike conventional currencies—which governments can print more of at will—Bitcoin has a fixed maximum supply.
